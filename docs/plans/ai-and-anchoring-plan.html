AI and blockchain anchoring: plan of action

All fourteen phases now work without AI or blockchain. This plan adds both as layers on top — AI that proposes and never decides, and anchoring that proves when a record existed — and sets out exactly what has to come from Kolkata Police before each step can start.

What we need from you

The engineering is the smaller part. These eight items decide the pace; the full list with detail is further down.

  1. Written authorisation for each AI useFrom Kolkata Police / the West Bengal Home Department, per use case. Face recognition and voice analysis need a separate legal opinion before any work starts.
  2. A data-protection impact assessment under the DPDP Act, 2023With a named data protection officer. It governs what data models may see and whether any of it may be used for tuning.
  3. GPU hardware at the MDC edge siteAll inference runs on premises. No case data goes to an external AI service. Starting size is given under Full requirements.
  4. Labelled examples from real workFIRs with correct BNS sections, audio with verified Bengali transcripts, annotated junction footage. Models are measured against these before an officer ever sees a suggestion.
  5. Officers' time for labelling and reviewA nodal officer who owns the programme, plus a small panel from investigation, cyber cell, traffic, control room and malkhana.
  6. A choice of anchoring targetLegal timestamping only, a government ledger through NIC, a consortium with partner agencies, or a public chain. The recommendation is below.
  7. Digital signature certificates for officersClass 3 DSC tokens or eSign, so custody transfers and approvals carry a personal signature, not only the platform's.
  8. Access agreements with outside systemsI4C / NCRP and the 1930 helpline, banks for fraud data, the CCTV management system, the body-worn camera vendor, CCTNS / ICJS.

Ground rules

These are already written into the platform plan and into the data model. Every workstream below is held to them.

A lead, never a conclusion

Nothing AI-generated becomes FIR content, an accusation, an arrest decision, a charge-sheet fact or an evidentiary finding without an officer's approval.

Confidence, sources, version

Every suggestion shows how sure the model is, the exact record text it relied on, and which model version produced it.

Recorded as AI

Accepted suggestions are stored with origin: ai and the approving officer — the column already exists on the relevant tables.

Refuse without a source

The knowledge assistant answers only from an authoritative document it can cite, and says so when none covers the question.

Places, never people

No person is scored, ranked or placed on a watchlist by a model. Risk work stays at the level of areas and time.

Data stays in jurisdiction

Models run on premises. No audio, footage, statement or personal data is sent to an outside AI provider.

Hashes on chain, never records

Anchoring publishes cryptographic fingerprints only. No personal data or case content ever leaves the platform.

Measured before enabled

A model is switched on for a module only after it meets a stated accuracy threshold on held-out examples, and each module has its own off switch.

AI workstreams

Ordered by risk and by what each unlocks. Codes are referenced in the sequence chart. Durations are indicative, for a small team of two ML engineers and one backend engineer, and assume data and approvals arrive on time.

A0

Foundations

Serves every moduleIndicative 4 weeks

  • An AI gateway inside the Go API as the only route to any model. It attaches the model version and the input's source records, and writes every suggestion to a review table with confidence, sources, status and reviewer.
  • A model registry and evaluation harness: held-out example sets per task, a stated threshold per module, and a switch that keeps a model off until it passes.
  • Monitoring of acceptance and override rates per station and per language, so a model that officers keep rejecting is visible and can be switched off.
  • Retire the three non-compliant prototypes above.

Prerequisites: the edge server with GPUs, permanent evidence storage, the role and permissions model, and a working automated test suite. None of these is optional for AI.

A1

Documents and language

Phases 11, 09, 05, core FIRIndicative 6–8 weeks

  • OCR for scanned Bengali and English documents, so scanned circulars become searchable by their text.
  • Semantic search and cited answers in the knowledge assistant, across English and Bengali (গ্রেপ্তারের পদ্ধতি finds the arrest SOP), refusing when no document covers the question.
  • Suggested BNS sections when an FIR is registered, each with the sentence that prompted it.
  • Complaint category, routing station and similar-complaint candidates for Phase 09.
  • Suggested phone numbers, UPI IDs and bank accounts pulled from cyber-fraud complaint text for Phase 05.

Why first: lowest risk — every suggestion is checked against text the officer is already reading — and useful every day.

A2

Investigation copilot

Phases 01, 12Indicative 6–8 weeks

  • A proposed chronology built from recorded statements, each event linked to the statement sentence it came from.
  • Possible contradictions between witnesses, shown side by side for the officer to accept or dismiss — the review workflow already exists.
  • Consistency checks across a case file before a submission pack is frozen, alongside the existing deterministic rules.
  • A draft case brief the IO edits and signs off.

Boundary: the model reads only the records of the case in front of the officer, and cites them. It never searches across other people's cases.

A3

Speech

Phases 13, 09Indicative 4–6 weeks

  • On-premises transcription of body-worn camera recordings and recorded complaints in Bengali, Hindi and English.
  • Speaker separation, so a transcript shows who spoke when.
  • Each transcript stored as a document derived from the signed recording, tied to its SHA-256, and never replacing it.
A4

Video

Phases 03, 06, 04Indicative 8–12 weeks

  • Vehicle and object detection on a first set of priority junction cameras, raised as events for operator triage — the triage workflow already exists.
  • Number-plate reading tuned for WB-series plates, feeding the traffic reconstruction plate-read register.
  • Natural-language search over detection records, purpose-logged like every footage search.
  • Speed estimates from calibrated cameras, always stored and shown as estimated, never as measured.
  • Appearance matching for missing persons by clothing and visible attributes.

Face recognition is not in this plan by default

It needs explicit legal authorisation, a proportionality assessment in line with the Supreme Court's privacy judgement, and a published policy on use and retention. Nothing here should be read as a commitment to build it.

A5

Operations analytics

Phases 05, 07, 08, 10Indicative 8 weeks or more

  • Workload forecasts for stations, so SHOs see pressure coming. This measures load, never individual performance.
  • Place-level incident trends shown with the factors behind them, extending the transparent Phase 10 scoring.
  • Suggested dispatch severity, with the operator deciding, and travel times by road routing instead of straight-line distance.
  • Mule-account indicators for fraud cases. These depend entirely on data from banks and I4C.

Blockchain anchoring

The platform is already tamper-evident: audit entries form a hash chain, custody transfers are signed, and evidence files are re-hashed on verification. Anchoring adds proof from outside the platform of when each state existed, so the police cannot be accused of rewriting their own history.

How a batch is anchored

CollectNew audit, custody, recording, malkhana and case-file hashes since the last batch.
CombineBuild a Merkle tree; one root stands for the whole batch.
AnchorSubmit only the root to the timestamping authority and the chosen ledger.
RecordStore the receipt against every row, filling the anchor columns that already exist.
ProveGive any record a proof a court or auditor can check without trusting the platform.
B0

Anchoring service and proofs

Covers audit trail, Phases 02, 06, 12, 13, 14Indicative 3 weeks

  • Batching on a schedule, a record of each batch, and anchor_batch_id, blockchain_anchor_tx and blockchain_anchor_time filled on each anchored row.
  • A proof endpoint and an offline verifier that checks a record against its anchor using only public information.
  • The anchor proof added to the court verification view in the evidence register.
B1

Legal timestamps

Needs a licensed timestamping authorityIndicative 1–2 weeks

  • RFC 3161 timestamps on each batch root from a timestamping authority licensed by India's Controller of Certifying Authorities.
  • This gives legally recognised proof of time under the IT Act, with no partner agencies and no blockchain.

B3

Personal digital signatures

Needs DSC tokens or eSign for officersIndicative 3–4 weeks

  • Officers sign custody transfers, submission-pack approvals and property disposals with their own certificate. This closes the Phase 02 gap where signatures prove only that the platform recorded an action.

What we will not build

Stated plainly, so nobody has to infer it.

  • Risk scores, rankings or watchlists of people, whether suspects, accused, complainants or officers.
  • Anything that takes an action by itself: registers an FIR, adds a charge, issues a warrant or closes a case.
  • Performance scores for individual officers built from workload or AI acceptance data.
  • Calls to external AI services with case data, audio, footage or personal information.
  • Personal data or case content written to any ledger. Only fingerprints are anchored.
  • Face recognition without explicit legal authorisation and a published policy.
